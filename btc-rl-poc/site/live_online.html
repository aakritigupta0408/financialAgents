The contract settles on whether the 60-second BRTI average at window close beats the window open. The kb arm calls it every minute — P(close ≥ strike) from t8's predicted distribution, calibrated per window phase. Brier (squared probability error, lower is better) scores us against the crowd on identical windows. Betting rule (matches the broker): exactly one bet per window — at most one, at least one — entry only under 80¢, settled at close. The arm picks its moment: it strikes the first minute its called side shows ≥5¢ of edge over the quote, or immediately if that side's price reaches 75¢ (about to cross the legality cap); with neither by the final 3 minutes it takes a forced entry (called side if under 85¢, otherwise the only legal side).

Every arm's live call the open window — eight models, side by side

Each arm scores the same open Kalshi window on its own. The big arrow is the arm's committed side; the bar underneath is its P(up) — the probability the price ends above the strike. For most arms the two agree, but the EV-trained arm (kb5) can bet against its own P(up) when the quote misprices the odds, so direction and P(up) may disagree by design. Direction calls — up or down, every slot

Two direction questions, one chart. The colored lines ask "up or down from the current price?" per horizon (consensus calls, 48 settled slots ≈ 4 h) — minute-scale BTC is near-martingale, so these hover at the coin flip by construction: the honest noise floor. The ivory line asks the well-posed question — "above or below a fixed strike at the window close?" (kb, market-anchored, rolled over ~10 windows) — and that one is predictive. Guides: coin flip 50%, 55% goal. Strips show the last 36 calls per horizon (▲ up / ▼ down · green right · red wrong · hollow pending). h15/h30 calls commit every 5 minutes into overlapping outcome windows, so their curves move in correlated steps.

Is it learning?

Two metrics, same +5-minute consensus calls. Level (solid blue): dollars of accuracy gained vs "predict no change" — a baseline that is near-optimal for price level, so this line is expected to hug zero. Direction (dashed teal): cumulative P&L of betting $1-per-$-move on the called side each slot — the metric where learned signal can actually pay. Ticks mark hourly retrains.
