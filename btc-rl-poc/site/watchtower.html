field guide

Not every problem is a SEV

Severity is a claim about consequences, not about noise. The taxonomy below decides how loudly a problem is allowed to ring.

SEV-0 The system could produce invalid or dangerous evidence. Anything that threatens the truthfulness of the experiment record — leakage, ledger corruption, a monitor certifying what it cannot see. Everything stops until the evidence is trustworthy again.
SEV-1 Major degradation of capability. A whole plane down — data capture dead, models unable to score, execution frozen. The record stays honest, but the machine is meaningfully diminished.
SEV-2 Important component degraded, system usable. A stale heartbeat, a wedged sync, a duplicate process. Most entries in the incident ledger live here — and most are repaired by the machine itself.
SEV-3 Minor. Cosmetic, observability gaps, paper cuts. Recorded anyway, because unrecorded problems are how big problems are born.
the lifecycle every SEV follows
Detection→ Triage→ Root cause→ Fix→ Verification→ Closure

Every incident above carries this whole chain in its record — including the two evidence-integrity incidents this system caught in its own experiment ledgers.
