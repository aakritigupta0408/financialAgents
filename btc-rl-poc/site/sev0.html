SEV-0 — where the error is born, live MITIGATING

A systematic bias (a built-in lean toward "price goes up") was traced to its origin and measured at every layer of the system. This page recomputes every number below from the raw ledgers on each refresh — the same data the daemon trades on. Full remediation spec with citations: docs/SEV0_REMEDIATION.md in the repo.

Executive summary — what happened, what was decided

The desk lost money on 08/26 while winning 65.6% of its bets, because break-even at the prices paid was 66.7%. The audit (tests/sev0_error_audit.py) walked the full architecture and found the loss was not one broken part but one small bias amplified by every layer above it. 95% of lost dollars carry a shared, taggable cause; only 5% were idiosyncratic.

The architecture, and how the error travels

Red dashes: the bias propagating up (its measured size at each tier updates live). Green dashes: mitigations pushing back. The chain: a stale "prices drift up" prior learned by the forecasting arms → inflated probabilities in the betting arms → an uncalibrated leader comparison that amplifies it → stake sizing that multiplies it.

Tier 2 — kb probability arms inheritance

AUC: does the arm rank winners above losers (0.5 = coin flip, 1 = oracle) · Brier: squared error of the stated probability (lower better; the market's own is shown for scale) · pBias: mean stated P(up) minus realized up-rate — positive = optimist · Platt a, b: the correction curve σ(a + b·logit(p)) that would make the arm honest (Platt 1999) — a calibrated arm has a≈0, b≈1; a<0 means "shift all its probabilities down".

Execution plan — the fix pipeline, with metric gates

Dependency-ordered: calibration (M1) is the unlock — the leader fix (M3) and asymmetric gate (M6) consume its output, so they wait. Each arrow is a metric gate: the measured number that must move, by how much, on how many windows, before the next step is trusted. Red branch = the pre-declared falsification path. Every behavior change runs in shadow mode first; every "X beats Y" claim needs a Diebold–Mariano test clustered by window.

Mitigation tracker

Ordered by leverage. Each carries its literature basis and a pre-registered acceptance metric — no fix is "done" until its metric clears on live windows.

M5
Sizing fixes — Saver 0.25→0.10; Gambler ≥0.77 gate + $10k stamped reset; MLE edge-margin 10¢ + shadow-row off-policy training.

M1
Per-arm online Platt layer — maintain (a,b) per arm, prequentially refit each settle; all cross-arm comparison moves to calibrated p_cal. Acceptance: |pBias| < 0.02 per arm and cross-arm AUC > 0.65 on trailing 100 windows.
Platt 1999 · Guo et al. 2017 (ICML) · prequential: Dawid 1984 · Brier decomposition: Murphy 1973
next up
M2
Knife-edge veto — no desk entry at |mkt−0.5| < 0.10 (41.6% wrong there; 52.8% of loss dollars). Acceptance: EV/$1 up with ≥60% coverage retained.
adverse selection near the pin: Glosten & Milgrom 1985
next up
M3
Fixed-Share leader — replace 10-bet churn with multiplicative expert weights + share rate (tracks the best sequence of leaders). Runs as a NEW desk treatment beside the Follower control. Acceptance: regret vs best fixed arm < 1%/$1 per 100 windows (churner today: 2.7%).

M4
Re-anchor T1 drift priors — per-arm trailing-bias recentering for t10 / t2@h30 / t11@h15 / t6; adaptive-conformal bands to restore 80% coverage. Acceptance: |bias| < $10 @h30; coverage 77–83%.

M6
Asymmetric gate while the lean persists — UP entries need p_cal ≥ τ+0.03 vs DOWN at τ (FP 33% vs FN 24%). Sunsets automatically when trailing pBias < 0.02.

Sources — the shelf this plan is built on

State of the art vs industry standard, and what we adopted or rejected for each, is argued in docs/SEV0_REMEDIATION.md. The shelf:

Online learning & selection · Herbster & Warmuth 1998 (Fixed-Share — adopted for M3) · Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi 2006 (experts framework) · Li et al. 2010, WWW (LinUCB — used in t2/t6/t10/t11) · Russo et al. 2018 (Thompson sampling) · Lattimore & Szepesvári 2020 (bandits — noted: our setting is full-information, so experts > bandits)

Drift & uncertainty · Gama et al. 2014, ACM CSUR (concept drift survey) · Adams & MacKay 2007 (BOCPD — rejected for now: regime sample count too small) · Romano et al. 2019, NeurIPS (CQR) · Gibbs & Candès 2021, NeurIPS (adaptive conformal — adopted for M4 bands) · Angelopoulos & Bates 2023 (conformal intro)

Markets & microstructure · Kyle 1985, Econometrica (informed trading / detection) · Glosten & Milgrom 1985, JFE (adverse selection — explains our knife-edge and ≥0.85-tail losses) · Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov 2014 (order-flow imbalance — adopted for M-T0) · Zhang, Zohren & Roberts 2019, IEEE TSP (DeepLOB — rejected: sample-starved at our scale)
